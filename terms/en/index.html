Maps and sovereignty

  • Uni Call's map exists only for navigation: it draws roads, street names, house numbers, one-way directions, railways, buildings, land use (schools, hospitals, commercial areas… colour only, no names), rivers, lakes and parks around your position, plus traffic camera locations and speed limits where OpenStreetMap data has them.
  • The only text on the map is street names and house numbers. The app shows no place names, country names, borders, administrative boundaries or maritime boundaries, and does not use or display the “nine-dash line” (“cow's tongue line”) or any territorial claim.
  • Inside Vietnam: the base map (roads, water, greenery) comes from Uni Call's own file made from OpenStreetMap data, with all place names and boundary lines removed at the data level; street names, house numbers, buildings, land use and one-way directions are added from OpenFreeMap (also OpenStreetMap data). Names in Chinese characters and any text inside the Hoàng Sa / Trường Sa area are removed. Outside Vietnam, or when that file is not yet available: the app reads data from OpenFreeMap and draws the same things.
  • The Hoàng Sa (Paracel) and Trường Sa (Spratly) archipelagos are Vietnamese territory. Place-search results in these archipelagos use Vietnamese place names (Hoàng Sa — Đà Nẵng, Trường Sa — Khánh Hoà) and never names or addresses according to another country's claim.
  • The “© OpenStreetMap” line is a mandatory condition of the data licence (credit to contributors), not a position on sovereignty. The app's map is not an official map and must not be used to determine borders or sovereignty.
